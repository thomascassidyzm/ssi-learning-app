# Which foreign languages Indian learners want — and why

*Market research for the India launch decision. 4 September 2026.*

**Headline: your hypothesis is half right.** Korean, Japanese, German and French all check out, each for a completely different reason. Chinese does not — demand in India is real but actively suppressed by geopolitics. And the language you didn't list, Arabic, turns out to be the most instructive negative result in the piece: the biggest migration corridor India has generates almost no language demand, because the Gulf runs on Hindi and English.

Every material claim below is tagged **MEASURED** (a number from a named, dated source) or **INFERRED** (my reasoning from those numbers).

---

## 1. The ranking, with drivers

### 1. German — the money language

- **Driver: labour migration, specifically nursing.** Germany's licence-recognition process requires **Goethe/telc B1 to start, B2 to practise** (only Hessen accepts B1 for full recognition). Nursing-specific exams exist (telc Deutsch B1·B2 Pflege, Goethe-Test PRO Pflege) and the number of telc-certified centres in India "has grown significantly in 2025–2026" (tern-group.com; winnyglobal, 2026). MEASURED on the requirement; the certification anchor is **B1/B2, medical-workplace register**.
- **Driver: study abroad.** ~**60,000 Indian students in Germany in 2025 — now the largest international student group there, overtaking China** (ICEF Monitor, 2025). MEASURED. Anchor: TestDaF/DSH ≈ B2–C1 for German-taught degrees, though many study in English.
- **Driver: employers at home.** Over 1,800 German companies operate in India (Bosch, Siemens, etc.); language specialists earn a 25–40% salary premium in BPO/MNC roles (industry sources, 2025). MEASURED-ish (industry claims, not government data).
- **Scale:** **154,000 people taking German classes in India** per the German Foreign Office's *Deutsch als Fremdsprache weltweit 2025* survey (published August 2025) — up from 18,550 schoolchildren in 2010. MEASURED.
- **Character:** exam-driven, visa-driven, high willingness to pay. This is the closest thing India has to a "German for nurses" product spec: get a Malayali or Punjabi nurse from zero to confident spoken B1/B2 German with clinical vocabulary.

### 2. Japanese — the quiet compounder

- **Driver: certification volume.** JLPT candidates in India: **10,739 in 2024, a record 11,014 in 2025**; New Delhi alone drew a record 6,061 (Asian Community News, 2025). MEASURED. Anchors: **N4 for the Specified Skilled Worker visa** (16 fields incl. care work), **N2 for Japanese-taught degrees and the skilled-professional points system**.
- **Driver: labour corridor — early but state-backed.** Only ~434 Indians currently hold SSW visas (undated recent figure — treat cautiously), but the bilateral target is **500,000 personnel exchanged over five years**, and Japanese care-work salaries run ~8× Indian nursing pay (MOFA/y-axis, 2023–25). MEASURED numbers, INFERRED trajectory: small base, strong tailwind.
- **Driver: culture.** Anime/manga is a genuine, durable acquisition driver; Japanese passed German to become #4 globally on Duolingo in 2025. MEASURED globally, INFERRED for India specifically.
- **Driver: IT corridor.** Japanese speakers earn a 30–40% premium in Indian IT/BPO due to Japan's labour shortage (industry sources, 2025).

### 3. Korean — the growth language

- **Driver: culture, overwhelmingly.** Korean is India's **fastest-growing foreign language**: **+75% in one year on Duolingo in India** (Duolingo 2024/25 reports), and by Duolingo's 2022 report it was already the most-learned language in India after English, Hindi and French. Globally Korean learner numbers track Hallyu exports almost exactly. MEASURED.
- **Driver: labour, emerging.** India joined Korea's Employment Permit System as a **pilot via NSDC**; the gate is the **EPS-TOPIK** exam, now administered at KVAC centres in seven Indian cities (2025). MEASURED that the pathway exists; scale unknown (see gaps).
- **Driver: employers.** Samsung, LG, Hyundai are large Indian employers; TOPIK enrolment has tripled globally since 2017.
- **Character:** young, mobile-first, daily-habit learners with listening-comprehension goals ("understand my group without subtitles") — a very different product from Goethe-B2 German, and the one best matched to a B2C awareness app. Anchor where exams matter: TOPIK I–II for study, EPS-TOPIK for labour.

### 4. French — the incumbent with a new engine

- **Driver: installed base.** French is the traditional "third language" of Indian schools — the 2022 Duolingo data put it ahead of Korean in absolute terms. Alliance Française de Madras alone enrols ~3,000 students a year across a 14-city network. MEASURED (order of magnitude).
- **Driver: Canada immigration — the new urgency.** TEF Canada / TCF Canada add **up to 50 CRS points** under Express Entry, and French-speaking streams are the softest route into a tightening Canada. Anchor: **CLB 7 ≈ B2 oral**. MEASURED on the mechanism.
- **Driver: France as a study destination.** Indian enrolments in France grew **17% to 9,100 in 2024/25; France's stated target is 30,000 Indian students** (ICEF Monitor, 2025). MEASURED.
- **Caveat:** the most crowded supply side of any language on this list — Alliance Française, schools, and every app compete here. INFERRED: hardest to differentiate in.

### 5. Spanish — big, soft, low-urgency

- Global #2 on Duolingo; a large hobby-and-schools base in India; steady Instituto Cervantes growth in Delhi (no published India enrolment figures — gap). BPO demand exists for Spanish-language roles serving US/LatAm customers.
- **No visa, exam, or migration anchor of any weight for Indians.** INFERRED: high volume, low willingness to pay, weak urgency. Fine passive catalogue item; not a promotion target.

### 6. Mandarin Chinese — real demand, actively suppressed *(contradicts your hypothesis — saying it early and plainly)*

- The 2020 National Education Policy **dropped Mandarin from the recommended foreign-languages list** amid the Ladakh standoff, and India put Confucius Institutes under Ministry of Education review (Deccan Herald, 2020; civilsdaily). MEASURED.
- Trade-driven demand persists (importers, manufacturing intermediaries), and HSK is still administered — but the institutional pipeline that feeds mass adoption (schools, universities, cultural centres) is throttled by policy, and 2024–26 sources describe interest as surviving *despite* geopolitics, not growing.
- INFERRED: Chinese in India is a niche professional purchase, not a wave. It does not merit one of the three promotion slots — **but see the known-side section: the `zho_for_hin` asset changes what "serving it" costs.**

### 7. Arabic — the corridor without a language market *(your unlisted one, checked properly)*

- The flow is enormous: ~**4M Indians in the UAE (2025), ~2.6M in Saudi Arabia, ~25% of Qatar's population** (Wikipedia/GLMM compilations, 2024–25). MEASURED.
- But the language demand does not follow. In Gulf workplaces **Hindi functions as the blue-collar lingua franca** (for Bangladeshis and Nepalis too), English as the white-collar one; the UAE even offers driving-licence tests in Hindi, Malayalam, Tamil and Bengali, and an Abu Dhabi labour court adopted Hindi (ThePrint; Wiley *World Englishes*, 2021). Indian migrants have thrived there for fifty years largely without Arabic. MEASURED on the sociolinguistics, INFERRED on the demand conclusion.
- **And the variant mismatch seals it:** what little demand exists is for **spoken Gulf dialect**; SSi's catalogue holds MSA, Egyptian and Levantine — none of which is the Arabic an Indian worker in Dubai would hear. Counting this as "covered" would be false.
- **Verdict: no build decision is triggered.** Arabic ranks high on corridor size and low on actual language demand. If SSi ever wants this market, the product is a *new* Gulf-Arabic course — a genuine build with a Gulf-dialect ear — and I'd want demand evidence first that doesn't currently exist.

### 8. Tail (one line each, cut rather than padded)

- **Russian:** ~25k Indian MBBS students study in Russia; demand is functional and modest. `rus_for_eng` is beta if ever wanted.
- **Italian:** small nursing/agri-work corridor to Italy; watch, don't act.
- **Striking side-finding:** the largest "language learning" market in India is **English itself plus Indian-to-Indian languages** — and SSi's nine *released* `eng_for_<Indian language>` courses are already its most built-out India asset. Outside this brief's scope, but it is the demand elephant in the room.

## 3. The known-side question

Should Indian learners get `X_for_eng`, `X_for_hin`, `X_for_tam`?

**The structural argument favours Indian known sides — more strongly than your articles example suggests.** Hindi and Chinese both lack articles, yes. But the bigger prize is word order: **Hindi and Tamil are SOV languages, and so are Korean and Japanese.** A Hindi speaker learning Korean keeps their sentence shape, their postposition-like case logic, and their article-free noun phrases; an English speaker has to invert all three. `kor_for_hin` is not just a cheaper cell — it is plausibly a pedagogically *better* course than `kor_for_eng` for a Hindi speaker. INFERRED, but from solid typology. Whoever part-built `kor_for_hin`, `zho_for_hin` and the Tamil twins was acting on exactly this.

**The market argument pushes the other way.** The paying, exam-driven segment — nurses, students, CRS-point chasers — is educated in English-medium systems and *expects* to be taught through English; every competitor (Duolingo, Goethe, Alliance Française) teaches them that way, so `X_for_eng` meets the buyer where they already are. The Hindi-medium and Tamil-medium mass market is larger in heads but unproven in willingness to pay for a *foreign* language (as opposed to English, where the nine released `eng_for_X` courses already serve them). **No data exists either way on this — I looked and it isn't there. This is the honest gap at the centre of the question.**

**Where the two arguments disagree is itself the finding**: for German and French (exam-anchored, English-medium buyers) there is no tension — English known side wins on both counts. For Korean (culture-anchored, mass-market, Hindi-belt K-drama audience is real) the structural and market arguments genuinely point in opposite directions — and SSi is the only player holding a finished-except-QA Korean-through-Hindi course with which to test it. The B2C awareness build is precisely the cheap instrument for that test: ship both, watch which one Hindi-belt users pick and finish.

**The multiplication problem, honestly:** every known-side cell needs its own ear and its own audio. The discipline should be: English known side is the default; an Indian known side is added only where (a) the audio already exists and (b) the target is SOV or otherwise structurally closer to the Indian language than to English. Today exactly four cells pass that filter — `kor_for_hin`, `zho_for_hin`, `kor_for_tam`, `zho_for_tam` — and two of them matter.

## 4. SO WHAT — the three cells

If only three cells get built or promoted for India:

1. **Promote `kor_for_eng`** (released today). Fastest-growing demand in the country, young mobile-first learners whose goal — comfortable listening — is what SSi's method actually produces. The natural face of a B2C awareness app.
2. **Finish and release `deu_for_eng`** (beta, 62k audio rows). The money language: nursing migration with a hard Goethe/telc B1–B2 anchor, the largest Indian student population in Europe, and the highest willingness to pay on the list. Spoken confidence is the notorious weak spot of exam-crammed German learners and the exact thing SSi sells. The cost is one German ear pass.
3. **QA and release `kor_for_hin`** (draft, content-complete, 43k audio rows). The differentiator nobody else has: Korean taught through Hindi, into the market where Korean is growing fastest, with a structural (SOV-to-SOV) advantage over every English-medium competitor. It also doubles as the live experiment that answers the known-side question with real users instead of speculation. The cost is one bilingual ear pass on work that is already recorded.

Free riders: `jpn_for_eng` and `fra_for_eng` are already released — list them prominently in the India app at zero marginal cost. Japanese narrowly missed the third slot only because promoting it costs nothing anyway; if a fourth slot existed it would be Japanese, on the strength of the JLPT numbers.

What I would *not* do: spend a slot on Spanish (no urgency anchor), on Mandarin-for-English (policy headwind), or on any Arabic (wrong variant, and the corridor demonstrably doesn't buy Arabic).

## 5. Gaps — what I could not establish

- **TOPIK and EPS-TOPIK registration numbers for India.** No published India-level figures found; "demand rising" comes from the Korean Cultural Centre's qualitative statements and Duolingo growth rates only.
- **Goethe exam candidate counts for India.** The 154,000-learners figure (AA survey, Aug 2025) is class enrolment, not exam volume; per-level exam data isn't published by country.
- **Instituto Cervantes India enrolments.** "Growing since 2009" is all that's public.
- **Scale of the India–Korea EPS pilot.** The pathway exists (NSDC pilot, KVAC centres in seven cities); how many Indians have actually gone is not published.
- **The SSW "434 Indians" figure is undated** in the source I found; treat as order-of-magnitude only.
- **Willingness of Hindi/Tamil-medium learners to pay for a non-English foreign language.** No data exists anywhere I could find. The `kor_for_hin` release is itself the cheapest way to generate it.
- **Whether `zho_for_hin`/`kor_for_hin` draft *quality* is releasable.** I verified depth (668 seeds, full phrase counts, tens of thousands of audio rows), not correctness — an ear pass is the gate, and status=draft may reflect real unfinished QA.
