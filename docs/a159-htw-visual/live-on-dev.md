## What changed

**The five drawings you liked are in.** What a go is, what a session feels like, what the listening stretches ask of you, say-it-before-you-hear-it, and the speaking-and-listening band. All restyled to match the pill and the player screen that shipped yesterday — same ink, same white card, no new colours, nothing moving. I checked all six on a 390-wide phone viewport against the real deployed build.

Changing course deliberately has no drawing. It is a directory row, and the real Library glyph at the bottom left is the thing to hunt for — a picture of a glyph teaches the wrong lookup.

**Every illustrated block got shorter.** That is the point of a drawing: three identical ticks say "getting it wrong still counts" faster than a sentence can argue it, so the sentence goes.

**The pace block is new.** It replaces *Thirty hours, spent however suits you* with **The best way to spend thirty hours**, and it leads with the recommendation rather than the menu: a big stretch, then a rhythm, then another big stretch. It names Easy and Fast, says plainly that Easy taken steadily is the better learning, and says just as plainly that five minutes a day for a year is far harder to actually keep up than it sounds. Fast is sold as *sooner*, never as better. The setting names do not move.

**The proof links are untouched** — the five rows, Croatian, Irish naming RTÉ Raidió na Gaeltachta, Japanese, Welsh, telly. No testimonials added.

## Small calls I made — say the word and any of them flips

- **Your "10 hour day" ships as "a whole day of it".** A stated ten hours reads as a dare rather than an invitation. One word to put the number back.
- **The Easy bullet in *Using the app* got a line stronger** — now "about double the thinking time and says each phrase twice over", to match the block that argues Easy is the better learning.
- **The pace block stayed as one block**, not two. It held together at that length.
- **The drawings' labels are English text inside the pictures.** A Welsh-known or Spanish-known learner would need every one translated, and "it turns a corner" may not carry. Nothing is broken today; it is a bill that arrives with the first non-English known language.
- **The thirty-hour number stays off the band drawing** — "it turns a corner" is the label, the number stays in the sentence underneath.
