*Think-piece, 2026-06-15. Captured from Aran's feedback at **day 45 of Croatian** — the first long run where the course goes a decent chunk beyond the Ireland (Irish) experience that the current seeds were redrafted from. Built to be argued with. **This is deliberately unformed: open questions and candidate loci, not solutions.** Aran's own framing — "we capture some of the thinking now, but it's unformed; we need dialogue on it before we have candidate solutions."*

**Status:** Think-piece — open. **Not start-now** by explicit decision (see §6). The pickup point is the next methodology cycle, around the next 7-day test.
**Companions:** `listening-layers.md` (Layer 1/2 — where the conversational material already lives and is accessible now); `adaptation-budget.md` §3 (already names *exactly* this layer — "the meta-communication layer: *I'm not sure how to say…*, *can you say that again*" — as the highest-impact, earliest material, criticality = introduction order); `metrics-architecture.md` (rate-of-change lens). ## 0. One-line frame

By ~day 45 the learner has the **functional blocks** cold (name, greetings, "nice to meet you") and will not crash on them. The gap is **what happens once you are actually *in* the conversation** — and it is not a gap in *ingredients*, it is a gap in **practice flows**: the rehearsed experience of *running* a live conversation, not just completing prompt→response cycles.

## 1. The observation (Aran, day 45)

- The pleasantries / "interests off before the conversation" are handled — listening has drilled them. No crash there.
- The felt gap is the moment *inside* the conversation: *"okay, what can I say… I'm gonna go for it… I'm not sure… I want… I can't remember."* The ingredients are all in there; what's missing is **conversational model flows** with them.
- These flows **do** exist in the **pods** — but the learner reaches them late (Aran hasn't got that far). They are *also* reachable in the **listening exercises right now**, so a motivated learner can do them before a trip. Aran intends to.
- Through his 40s he's been thinking *"I'm ready for this stuff,"* and it hasn't arrived in the listening: *"where the fuck is it?"*

## 2. The reframe

Not *"missing ingredients."* It's *"what are the practice flows that mean I hit the ground running in conversation #1?"*

And explicitly **not make-or-break**: the first real conversation does the heavy lifting by itself — *"if I stayed in Croatia for 2 hours now, I'm absolutely certain by the end I'll be doing really well."* So this is **cherries on the cake** — but real cherries, worth thinking about.

## 3. The central unresolved question — *where does the fix live?*

Aran moves between three loci and does **not** settle on one. Naming the cut is the first thing the dialogue has to do:

1. **Speech-side priming.** Early framing: don't necessarily change the listening — prime for the conversation *in the speech*. (Most of §4 lives here.)
2. **Listening pods.** Closing framing: *"is the next thing to escalate the listening pods toward that rather than change seeds? Quite possibly."*
3. **Seeds.** A sense that the seeds are thin on what the *other* person says (see §5).

These aren't mutually exclusive, but the locus changes what we'd build. **Resolving this is prerequisite to any candidate solution.**

## 4. Mechanisms floated (speech-side)

Currently the speech runs prompts as an **unpaired, methodological prompt→response cycle**. Two additions came up:

- **Prompt pairing / conversational connective tissue.** Scaffold the *managing* of a conversation, not just the answering. Aran's example pair:
  - *"Now we've started talking — I'm not sure what to say."*
  - → *"It's okay, take your time. Would you like to talk about fish?"*
  - i.e. stall-handling, topic-offering, turn-keeping — the glue between prompts.
- **Target-language prompt → target-language response.** Tom's old idea from the first intensive days: by ~8 hours, the engine *knows what the learner has covered*, so it can issue a **TL prompt the learner will recognise** (because they've drilled the response several times) and they'll know they can answer it.
  - **The snag:** it's not easy to model *the* response — there isn't one right answer.
  - **The resolution Aran reaches:** don't model one — *"you said something; you could have said one of these three things"* → and **that itself becomes a listening exercise.** (A neat loop: speech TL-prompt → learner attempts → multiple model responses fed back as listening.)

> Cross-link: `adaptation-budget.md` §3 already argues that this meta-communication layer is the *highest-criticality, earliest-introduced* material (criticality = introduction order, not frequency). If that's right, conversational-flow material isn't an add-on at the end — it's structurally early. Worth reconciling the two papers.

## 5. The seeds gap — interlocutor turns are thin

The seeds carry plenty of *learner* lines but are light on **what the other person says** in a real conversation. Present: *"I used to be really good…", "how long have you been there?", "I think you're doing really well."* Missing categories Aran named:

- **Conversation-management from the other side:** *"I've been nodding for a while, I don't want to stop talking now."*
- **Motivation questions + natural answers:** *"Why did you want to learn?"* → *"Well, I thought it'd be interesting… I wanted to come on holiday and speak a few words."*
- General holiday / small-talk texture.

**Why now:** the seeds were redrafted off the **Ireland** experience and the shortfalls picked up there; Croatian is reaching a chunk **beyond** Ireland, which is exactly why this next layer of thinness is surfacing.

## 6. A past-tense practice idea (smaller, concrete)

Croatian past tense via the *"-o ending"* (the masculine active past participle). Aran's example chain on *trebati* (to need): **trebam** (I need) → **trebao** (needed) → **trebao bih** (… *should / would need*). Idea: **group several of these together** and pull other verbs into the same past tense for nice extra practice.

> **Data note for whoever touches this** (do *not* correct Aran's casual spoken example — this is a flag for the hrv content): *trebao bih* is **present conditional** — "I should / I would need" — not "I should have." Given the ZUT 1:1-mapping doctrine, confirm the hrv glosses don't collide present-conditional with past. (See the ZUT/pair-contract discipline in the course-builder gates.)

## 7. The sequencing decision — **don't work on this now**

Tom's explicit call. This would be a *bad* thing to build right now. The order stands:

1. Ship **schools** and **freelancers**.
2. Get freelancers **operating** — producing and publishing from the property.
3. From there, in a position to **chase podcasts to go on**.
4. *Then* work on the **methodology** — and run the **next 7-day test**.
5. The 7-day test turns this unformed thinking into candidate solutions → think / rebuild / additional stuff → **another 7-day test** to see if it's nailed.

The 7-day test is the instrument that forms the ideas; theorising ahead of it is premature. *("By that point I think we'll have something pretty fucking special — well, it already is incredibly good.")*
