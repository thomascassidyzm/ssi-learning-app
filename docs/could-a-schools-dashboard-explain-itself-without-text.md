# Could a schools dashboard explain itself without text?

*A position, argued against the twelve live schools surfaces. Fable, 2026-09-10. Nothing here is a design and nothing here executes.*

## The verdict, first

Partly, and the boundary is sharp enough to name. About a quarter of what the schools Handbook describes is a **reading**: a fact the page shows. For readings the answer is yes, and the product has already done most of it. Roughly three quarters is a **verb**: a button that does something. For verbs the answer is no, and it is a no for a structural reason, not a taste one. The meaning of a verb is its consequence, and a consequence is not on the screen. It is in the future, in somebody else's inbox, on a card statement, on another class's roster. No drawing reaches there.

But the frame the question generates is more useful than the yes or the no. The design debt is not *text*. The debt is **second artefacts**: anything that is *about* the surface rather than *being* the surface. A walkthrough clip is a second artefact. A Handbook sentence is a second artefact. A belt strip is not, because it is the data. And, critically, the impact list in the delete modal is not either, because it is computed from the same state the delete handler reads. That last one is the move. The text does not go away. It stops being an explanation and becomes evidence, which cannot go stale because it is not a copy of anything. Once you see the 72 uncovered capabilities through that lens they stop being one queue and become three, and only the smallest of the three wants a clip.

## 1. What the twelve surfaces are actually made of

I read the templates and the HANDBOOK comments of all twelve, plus the two the commission's list implied: the analytics route mounts `insight/TeacherInsightsView.vue` for a teacher and redirects a leader to NodeInsightsView, and the verbs on node-home live in `NodeActionBar.vue`, which carries ten capability descriptions of its own. No surface Tom named was missing. AdminInvites is a hundred-line shell whose real content is three child components I did not open, so it is the one surface where I argue from its role rather than its controls.

Sorting every capability I read by kind, two things stand out.

**The product has already drawn its readings.** Bench bars for class against school against global, a journey bar in LEGOs, a belt strip, a seven-day sparkline, a health dot, a map rail whose depth is an indent, a setup rail where a done step is a tick and an undone step is its number, and an empty roster drawn as six empty seats. Three of those are Tom's own "absence is drawn, not coloured" rule, alive in schools before it was written down for Popty. The bench shows a dash rather than an invented bar when the data is too thin. The rail's undone step has no fill. The empty class is empty places, not a heading over a sentence.

**Every clip the estate has written is on a verb.** The twelve schools clips hang off run-class-session, move-a-teacher, hand-over-the-lead, share-a-class, invite-a-supply-teacher, invite-first-person and their kin. Nobody wrote a clip for "reading the belts" or "how fresh these numbers are". The authors' instinct already knew where explanation was needed, and it was never the readings. That instinct is the finding in miniature.

## 2. Where drawing genuinely carries the meaning

Named, on the surfaces, with the reason each works.

**The class roster's per-learner position.** ClassDetail and the node-home student list. The fact is a quantity on a known scale, LEGOs out of a course total, and the eye reads a length faster than it reads a fraction. The journey bar already does this. The number beside it is a legend, not an explanation, and I return to that distinction below.

**The three-bar bench.** Dashboard and class cards. "Is this class normal" is a ratio question, and three bars of different length answer it before the reader has found the axis. The HANDBOOK text for it is four sentences describing which bar is which. A legend of three words does the same job and needs no sentence.

**Belt distribution.** The strip on ClassDetail and node-home. "A wide band of one colour means everyone is together" is a shape fact, and shape is drawing's home ground. This is also the one colour semantics schools already has, and the HANDBOOK is proud of it: "every screen in the product uses that one ladder, so a student never shows a different belt in two places". That consistency is what makes a channel learnable at all.

**Absence of a state.** The six empty seats. The undone setup step. The dash in the bench. All three are Tom's rule already generalised to schools, and all three work because absence of a *state* has a natural drawing: the outline of the thing that would be there.

**Recency and staleness.** "Last active" on every roster, and the "Updated 09:41" stamp above the numbers. Both are time facts, and Tom's second ruling already assigns time a channel: would time fix this, then grey. I am not proposing how to draw them, only observing that they belong to the class of facts his grammar has a slot for.

**Transient state on a control.** "Copied!" on a link chip, "Saving…" on a button, the disabled seat-update button reading "(current)" so you cannot double-bill yourself. These are already drawn as class changes with a word alongside. The drawing carries the state; the word carries it to a screen reader.

That is the whole of category (a), and notice its shape: every item is a **quantity, a position on a known ladder, or the absence of one**. Nothing in it is a verb.

## 3. Where it provably cannot, control by control

**The domain list on Settings.** "Which addresses look like your school". Its meaning is a double negative that the surface has to state in forty words: it lets nobody in, keeps nobody out, and only changes the *order* of another page's list. A drawn list of domains says "these are your domains", and the universal reading of that drawing is an allowlist, which is exactly the wrong reading. A fact whose entire content is what it *does not* do cannot be carried by presence. Presence asserts. This is the cleanest counter-example in the product and it is not an edge case: three of the seven Settings capabilities are this list.

**The access code.** TeachersView and node-home. The code works once, lasts two days, and whoever uses it *becomes that teacher*, so do not post it anywhere shared. A code is a code. Expiry is a time fact and drawable in principle. Impersonation is a consequence to a third party and there is no channel for "the next person to type this will be Sioned". The caveat sentence is not decoration. It is the thing the feature's safety depends on.

**Remove versus Leave.** The same button on the ClassDetail teacher row changes its verb by who you are: a lead or a leader sees Remove, a co-teacher sees Leave. And the co-teacher sees no "Add another teacher" and no "Make lead", with a sentence in their place: "Only the lead teacher or a school leader can bring another colleague in." A textless surface shows the co-teacher a class with no way to add a colleague and no way to know a way exists. Tom's "absence is drawn" covers the absence of a *state*. It does not cover the absence of a *capability*, because you cannot draw the button that is not there without drawing it, and the product's whole philosophy on the Teachers page is that you show people what they can do and say in words who can do the rest. Put through his own tiebreak: would money fix it, lock. Would time fix it, grey. Would *role* fix it, and there is no channel assigned. That is a genuine hole in the grammar, not a gap in my reading of it, and I name it as one of the two things for Tom at the end.

**Delete.** The class bin on ClassDetail, "Delete" on the node action bar, "Delete school" under Danger zone. The bin is already icon-only, and it is one of only two textless controls on the class page. Notice which two: the two that need text least on the button and most at the moment of commitment. The bin carries "delete". It cannot carry "Year 7 French takes 23 students and 14 sessions with it, and this cannot be undone". The modal carries that, as computed impact lines, and when there is real activity it demands the class name typed back. The typed name is the actual safety, and it is text by construction: you cannot type a drawing. Every product draws irreversibility as a warning triangle, and every user has learned to click through the triangle. The product's own design says so: the typed confirm exists *because* the triangle does not work.

**Subscribe.** UpgradeView. The button reads "Subscribe — £84/mo". The money is on the face of the button, and that is the honesty. Below it the seats-versus-joined line is a pair of quantities, which is drawable, but its meaning is a policy: nothing is blocked, nobody is locked out, "we would rather show you the gap than shut a class out mid-lesson". Draw "5 over" in red and every reader on earth reads a gate. Here a drawing would not merely fail to carry the meaning. It would carry the opposite meaning. That is a stronger objection than "cannot", and it recurs wherever the product's policy is deliberately gentler than the convention the drawing would invoke.

**Proper nouns.** The class name, the school name, "Taught by Sioned Jones (lead)", the join code, the course name. Identity is text and the most important text on the page is the one Welsh-medium school name that no translation and no icon touches. The map rail draws depth as an indent and carries every node as a name at that depth, which is the right division. And course names are where the product has already run the experiment: Settings carries a toggle "Show flags on courses", off by default. Flags are the obvious icon language for languages and they fail, because flags are countries. Spanish for English speakers, Latin American accent, has no flag. Catalan has a flag its neighbours dispute. Breton has one almost nobody recognises. The product made flags optional because it found this out.

**Consequence elsewhere.** After you add a pupil on ClassDetail, a note says they are "still in" their other class and links to it. That consequence lives on a different roster. The seat stepper opens at the number of teachers who have actually joined. The "Not yet given classes" section on TeachersView sorts an unexpected arrival to the top, which is a *position* fact and drawable, but what the position means, that they can sign in and learn but see no learner until you tick a class, is a policy sentence again.

Pulling these together, the resisters are not four categories. They are one: **a verb's meaning is downstream of the tap**, and the screen only shows upstream. Consequence, permission, irreversibility, policy and third-party effect are all the same thing seen from different sides.

## 4. What a system of visual semantics for schools would have to commit to

Tom's three rulings are a partial grammar for a *reading* surface, and they transfer to schools readings almost untouched. Here is what happens to each, and what has to join them.

**Colour means measurement and nothing else.** Transfers, and schools currently violates it in the exact way Popty did. The belt dot on every class row of the teacher dashboard is hard-coded white. It is a measurement's clothes on a decoration, so the eye learns that belt colour on this page means nothing, and then has to unlearn it on the roster. The brand red is the kicker colour on AdminInvites. Delete is red. "Trial ended" is a pill. "Awaiting admin" is a pill. Colour in schools currently does brand, measurement, danger and state, which is the pig's breakfast at a smaller scale. One consequence needs Tom's ear and I put it in the decision list: red for a destructive verb is the single most universally read colour convention there is, and it is not a measurement. Either it is the principled exception or colour-means-measurement bends.

**Absence is drawn, not coloured.** Transfers cleanly and is already there three times. Nothing to add.

**ONE FACT ONE CHANNEL, and on collision the fact that will not resolve itself wins.** Transfers to readings. Breaks at verbs, twice. First, a verb has one channel, its label, and that label already has to carry a proper noun *and* a consequence: "Make lead", "Remove", "Leave", "Update to 12 seats — £96/mo". Two facts on one channel is the collision the rule forbids, and there is no second channel on a button to move one of them to. Second, the tiebreak has two cases, money and time, and schools needs a third: role. Would role fix it has no channel. So the rule generalises to everything in section 2 and bounds out exactly at section 3, which is the same boundary drawn a second way, and that is some evidence the boundary is real.

**What has to join them: evidence over explanation.** This is the commitment the frame forces, and it is the one that actually answers Tom's worry about clips lying. For a reading, the drawing *is* the data, so it cannot be stale. For a verb, the equivalent is a **computed consequence at the moment of commitment**, derived from the same state the handler reads. The delete modal's impact lines. The price on the subscribe button. The "still in" note. The seat stepper seeded from the real joined count. All of those are text, and none of them can drift from what the button does, because they are not a description of the handler, they are the handler's preview. A clip that says "tapping this deletes the class" is a second artefact and it will lie the day the handler changes. A modal that says "deleting Year 7 French takes 23 students" cannot lie, because if the handler stops taking students with it the number changes with it.

So the commitment is: **a verb ships its consequence or it is not a verb.** How a new capability gets its semantics without an author inventing one falls out directly. If it is a reading, it draws on one of the assigned channels or it does not draw. If it is a verb, its consequence is computed and shown at commit. Neither needs a description written and neither can go stale.

**Legends, not explanations.** The word beside the belt dot, the "Class / School / Global" beside the bench, the health word beside the health dot: these teach the channel once and they are what a screen reader gets. They stay. The tip cards on TeachersView, "Admins manage staff, classes and settings. Teachers see only their own classes", are explanations, and they go, because the surface they explain already shows who has which buttons. The test is whether the words are *about* a channel or *about* the page.

**What it costs to keep.** Here I have to be honest against the strong form of the case. Colour-means-measurement cannot be linted. Nobody can write the gate that catches a white belt dot used as decoration, and Tom's Popty diagnosis shows how fast the rule decays without a gate: every colour doing three jobs, on a product he built himself. So the grammar for readings is a review discipline, and its running cost is one pair of eyes on every schools change. The evidence commitment for verbs is different. That one is code, it ships with the handler, and its running cost is nothing beyond the handler, which is the whole attraction.

## 5. The failure modes, honestly

**Icon literacy.** Look at the verb bar on node-home: Invite a person, Get a shareable link, Add a group, Add a school, Add a class, Mint a demo org, Courses, Rename, Delete. Nine verbs, all words. There is no icon for "a shareable link by role" that is distinguishable from "a personal link for a named person", and that distinction is the whole point of having two verbs. The only icon-only controls in the twelve surfaces are the pencil, the bin, the cross on a draft row and a home glyph on the rail, which is roughly the entire set of icons a school administrator can be assumed to read. The play triangle is next, and even that carries "as class" in words because "play" alone is the wrong promise.

**Cultural variance.** The flags toggle is the product's own evidence. The belt ladder is the subtler one: white, yellow, orange, green, blue, purple, brown, black is a martial-arts order, and a teacher who has never been near a dojo cannot infer from the colours that purple is above blue or brown above purple. The HANDBOOK spells the whole ladder out with its thresholds for exactly that reason. So the product's strongest colour semantics still needs its legend, and it is a legend the reader has to be told once rather than one they can derive.

**Discoverability.** A textless verb is undiscoverable, and on a phone there is no hover to rescue it. The estate has a documented case with text present: on 2026-08-08 Tom went looking for the class teachers panel on staging and concluded the feature did not exist, because it was fourth in the rail and last in reading order on a phone. Words did not save it. Their absence would have buried it further.

**Screen readers, and procurement.** The current practice in the code is instructive. HealthDot carries an `aria-label`. BeltStrip is `role="img"` with a label. The six empty seats are `aria-hidden` and the sentence beside them carries the meaning. The product already does "drawing for the eye, text for the reader". A purely visual semantics is by construction unavailable to a blind school administrator, and this product is sold to schools and to a government. Public-sector accessibility regulation in the UK requires WCAG AA of public bodies and of what they procure, and the government contract is half the revenue target in this repo's own business model. A textless surface would not pass procurement, full stop. The honest resolution is the one already half-built: the text stays as the accessible name and the legend, and stops being the visible explanation. That is not a compromise on the idea. It is the only form in which the idea can be sold to the customer it is for.

**Drawings that lie.** Section 3's overage case. Wherever the product's policy is gentler than the convention, red overage that is not a gate, a "needs attention" that the HANDBOOK insists is "a prompt to look rather than a verdict", the drawing imports the convention and overrides the policy. A channel carries the meaning the reader already has for it, not the one you assign.

**Channel exhaustion.** Schools has perhaps four usable channels on a card: colour, fill against outline, weight or opacity, and position. It has more facts than that on a single teacher row: role, lead, pending invite, is-me, own practice, student hours, status. Grey alone is already doing four jobs in schools: disabled, stale time, inert ancestor on the rail, and the "subtle" text class. That is the Popty failure arriving by a different door, and it is why ONE FACT ONE CHANNEL is a scarcity rule before it is a clarity rule.

**The quiet dot.** The HANDBOOK describes the health indicator as "a quiet coloured dot". It is designed to be unobtrusive, and rightly, because it is a hedge. A semantics that has to be quiet cannot also be the explanation.

## 6. The third thing: the 72 are three queues

The question on the table is 72 clips or a redesign, and the surfaces say it is a false pair. Split the uncovered capabilities by kind and each kind wants something different, and only one of them wants a clip.

**Readings.** On the order of twenty of what I read: the numbers on any level, the freshness stamp, the bench, the journey, the belts, per-student-per-week, one student's progress, the roster, look up a student, the schools table, voice and pause, the class against the average. Not one of these needs a clip. What they need is the reading grammar held: the white belt dot fixed, colour reserved for measurement, one legend per channel. Three of the nine bare surfaces, dashboard, students and analytics, are almost entirely readings. They are bare because nobody felt the lack.

**Verbs with a consequence the surface can compute.** The majority. Delete, remove, subscribe, change seats, add a student, assign classes, hand over the lead, rename, add a class. These do not want a clip either, because a clip is a stale preview of what the commit moment should show live. Several already do it well. The work is to make the rest do it, and to treat a verb without a computed consequence as unfinished rather than undocumented.

**The residue: policy, permission-absence and place.** The domain list. The access code's lifetime and impersonation rule. Choosing what role someone arrives as. Trial durations under Courses. The seats-over policy. Setup at your own pace. That the class link stays valid in week six. Who can add a colleague. Where Delete school lives. On the order of a dozen of what I read, and this is my reading, not a census. This is the only place a sentence or a clip earns its keep, because the fact is neither on the screen nor at the commit. It is also the only place the staleness problem actually bites, since a computed consequence cannot drift and a drawn reading cannot drift. Whatever Tom decides about freshness applies to this dozen, not to 72.

**What I would do, stated as a position.** Stop treating "72 without a clip" as the number. Hold the reading grammar as a review rule and fix the two violations already visible. Make "a verb ships its consequence" the standing rule for schools verbs, so the safety and the explanation are the same computed thing. Then look at the residue with fresh eyes and decide, capability by capability, whether it wants a clip, a sentence, or a change to the product so the policy stops being surprising. The domain list is a good candidate for the third: a control that has to say "this grants nothing" in forty words may be a control that should not be on a school administrator's page at all.

**Two things only Tom can rule on.**

1. **Red for Delete.** Colour-means-measurement says no. The most universally literate colour convention in software says yes. Principled exception, or bend the rule?
2. **The role channel.** Money gets the lock, time gets grey, and role gets nothing. Either permission-absence stays invisible by design, with the one derived sentence saying who can, which is what the product does today and what I would keep, or it gets a channel, and that is a design decision I have deliberately not made.

## Gaps, and defaults I took

- **AdminInvites** is a shell around three child components I did not open. My reading of it is by role, not by control. No other surface is inferred.
- The kind-split proportions in section 6 are from the capabilities I read in the twelve surfaces and their mounted components, not from the 86-capability census, which I did not recount as instructed.
- The retired `self-explaining-dashboard.md` is cited by live code on node-home. I read its headings only, to confirm it asks about noticing rules and a compiled explainer, not about textless surfaces. The pre-flight claim that nobody has asked this question stands.
- **Length.** The brief's default was fifteen minutes. This is closer to twenty because the argument is the named controls, and vagueness was named as the enemy.
- **Location.** The brief said `docs/`, so it is there, although this repo's CLAUDE.md retired that tree. The brief is the ruling for this job.
