Listening · Layer 1

Little and often:
the 30-cup wheel.

Maintenance listening used to arrive as a rare ten-minute block. Now it arrives as a minute at the end of every round — thirty little cups of listening on a wheel, one poured each round, each sentence served in a sandwich that carries its own meaning.

Status: live in the app Read: ~4 minutes
01 · The shape

Thirty cups, one pour per round

A dose small enough to never feel like an interruption, frequent enough to never let the material go cold.

Picture thirty cups on a wheel. At the end of every round of speaking practice, one cup is poured: roughly a minute of listening. Over thirty rounds the wheel turns full circle, then starts again. That's the whole rhythm — little and often, woven into the practice itself, instead of an occasional listening session you have to remember to do.

What goes in the cups? Sentences you have already fully met — every part of them introduced and produced. The cups fill gradually as you advance:

  • Nothing fires until you've met your first thirty sentences. Listening earns its place; it never front-runs the speaking.
  • From there, every further thirty sentences you're introduced to adds one sentence to every cup — the doses grow as your repertoire grows.
  • The wheel tops out at twenty sentences per cup. On a long course, the listening layer deliberately maintains the foundational material; the newest sentences are still being carried by active practice and don't need it yet.

And when a course ends — no new material arriving — the wheel simply keeps turning over what it holds. Steady background maintenance, forever. That's not a limitation; it's the design. Fluency in what you know beats a fading acquaintance with more.

02 · The serving

The comprehensible-input sandwich

Every sentence in the cup plays four times — and the second play is the one doing the quiet heavy lifting.

1 · targetHear it1× · voice one
2 · your languageHear its meaning1×
3 · targetHear it, understood1× · voice two
4 · targetStretch2× speed

One sandwich per sentence, in cup order. No text on screen at any point.

Hear the sentence. Hear what it means, in your own language. Hear the sentence again — now understood. Then once more at double speed, a stretch rep that pulls your ear ahead of comfortable. The middle slot is what makes the input comprehensible: without it, a replayed sentence is just sound; with it, every replay lands as meaning.

The 2× play isn't showing off. Train the ear faster than real life, and real life starts to sound slow.

03 · Working in the open

Why the meaning came back

We publish the wrong turns too, because the wrong turns are where the design earns its shape.

What actually happened

The cup went through several target-only designs first: a decay ladder where each sentence sped up as it aged; a flat once-slow-once-fast pour; then a slow-only version, on the theory that fast replays without scaffolding were just fast noise.

Road-testing showed the real problem was deeper than speed. A sentence pulled out of its conversation has no surrounding flow to infer meaning from — so pure target-language replays, at any speed, teach nothing once the memory has faded even slightly. The fix wasn't a speed. It was restoring the meaning: the sandwich, with your own language in the middle. The moment that slot came back, the whole layer started doing its job.

One refinement is still arriving: the sentences in each cup are being regrouped into authored clusters — linguistically curated sets, chosen by a human course author so that each cup quietly reinforces a pattern rather than being an arbitrary handful. The wheel runs today with a sensible automatic grouping; the authored clusters slot in as they're written, course by course.
