An open question

Hitting the ground running in conversation #1.

By day 45, a learner has the ingredients for their first real conversation — and won't crash on the pleasantries. The gap we've found is subtler: not missing words, but missing flows. This page is thinking in progress, published before it's finished, because that's how the method has always been built.

Status: open design question — not yet built Read: ~4 minutes
01 · The observation

Day 45 of Croatian

The best test data we have is ourselves. One of our founders learns a new language with the method every couple of years, from zero, and reports from the inside.

Forty-five days into Croatian, the report was precise. The functional blocks were cold-solid: name, greetings, "nice to meet you", the on-ramp material a first conversation opens with. Listening practice had drilled them; no crash coming there.

The felt gap was the moment inside the conversation — the live scramble of "okay, what can I say… I'm going to go for it… I'm not sure… I want… I can't remember." Every ingredient needed for that moment was already in the course. What was missing was the rehearsed experience of running a conversation with them: managing it, steering it, surviving its wobbles — rather than completing one prompt–response cycle at a time.

The gap isn't in the ingredients. It's in the flows.

Worth saying plainly: this is not make-or-break. The first real conversation does enormous heavy lifting all by itself — two hours in-country and a learner at this stage will be doing really well by the end of them. This is about arriving at that conversation already warmed up. Cherries on the cake — but real cherries.

02 · Candidate mechanisms

Two ideas on the table

Neither is settled. Both are the kind of thing the method can test properly, so we will.

Conversational connective tissue

Today's practice runs prompts as independent cycles. The idea: pair them, so the learner rehearses the glue of a conversation — stall-handling, topic-offering, keeping a turn alive — not just the answers. For example:

You"Now we've started talking — I'm not sure what to say."
Them"It's okay, take your time. Would you like to talk about fish?"

That exchange teaches nothing new lexically. What it rehearses is the move: being stuck, saying so in the language, and being handed a way forward. Learners who have practised being stuck don't freeze when it happens for real.

Prompts in the target language — with three right answers

The engine knows exactly what a learner has covered. So by a few hours in, it can ask a question in the target language that the learner will recognise — because they've drilled answers to it many times. The snag is that a real question has no single right answer. The resolution we like: don't pretend it does. The learner says something — and then hears "you could have said any of these three", and those three model answers become a listening exercise. The attempt and the feedback loop into each other.

03 · What's genuinely unresolved

Where does the fix live?

The honest state of the thinking, stated as a fork.

Locus A

In the speaking practice

Prime for conversation inside the prompt cycles themselves — paired prompts, connective tissue, target-language questions.

Locus B

In the listening

Escalate the conversational listening material toward full model flows — hear whole conversations being run, wobbles and all, before you run one.

There's a third suspicion behind both: the course material carries plenty of what the learner says, and rather less of what the other person says — the questions they'll ask you, the encouragement they'll offer, the way they'll hand you a topic. Any full answer probably grows that side too.

How this gets resolved

Not by theorising. The method's instrument for questions like this is the intensive self-test: a founder takes a brand-new language into a compressed run, and the design question rides along as something to observe, not argue about. This question is queued for exactly that. Until then it stays deliberately unformed — publishing it in this state is the point. An approach that only shows its finished answers isn't showing you its method.
