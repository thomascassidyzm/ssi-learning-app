Position paper · for Tom & Aran

The insight engine.
Now we can ask anything.

For the first time, both halves are in place: we capture everything, and we can interrogate it — neither was possible before. This is the delivery system that turns that into insight, where a new question is a composition, not a project — reading the measurement model of Measuring Progress, not a new one.

01 — The opportunity

Two things were always missing. Both are now in place.

For the first time, we get to build this right — from the ground up.

Real insight needs two things, and for most of SSi's life we had neither. We couldn't reliably capture what learners actually did — the telemetry simply wasn't there. And even where data existed, we had no way to interrogate it without a bespoke build for every question. Either gap alone is fatal.

Both are now solved — and that's the genuinely new thing. We capture everything: tens of thousands of events a month, nearly every one tagged on every axis at once — content, learner, cohort, region, device, build, time. And the means to ask it anything is exactly what this paper builds. We're, for once, in the rare and happy position of designing the interrogation layer from scratch, on a full data substrate.

A new insight becomes a composition, not a project — and that's the whole leap to world-class.

The opportunity is a generous one: not "a better dashboard" (a dashboard is only a cheaper answer to a question someone already chose), but driving the cost of asking a new question toward zero. The substrate is here; what we get to build is the engine that lets us ask.

02 — Coherence

The same substrate as Measuring Progress — a new surface on it.

One theory of measurement; one engine that serves it everywhere.

This paper introduces no competing theory — it would be a failure if it did. Measuring Progress defines what we measure: the difficulty × execution coordinate, the discipline of reading curvature not level, the self / group / world frames, the sovereign comparison rule, the layered architecture. The insight engine is the system that reads that model. Its whole vocabulary is the measurement model's:

  • its metrics are the measured signals — retention, friction, calibration, hours, conversion;
  • its frames are Measuring Progress's self / group / world / content;
  • its order is Measuring Progress's level / velocity / acceleration.

Nothing is invented. The measurement model is simply made queryable, composable, and self-interpreting — so that a number a learner sees and a number an admin sees are the same definition at different GROUP BY levels.

03 — Architecture

Three layers. The first changes everything; the third leads the world.

01

Substrate

the WHAT, as config

Entities × metrics × dimensions × frames × order, declared once — not buried in one-off SQL. Any question becomes a row: {metric} × {entity} × {frame} × {order} × {window}. Define a metric once, tested; two surfaces can never quietly disagree about what "retention" means.

02

Query surface

ask in plain language

Natural language in → mapped to a composition over the registry → an answer back. The team stops waiting for a chart and starts asking. Saved compositions become the boards — bookmarks, not software.

03

Interpreter

an analyst in the box

Every other tool hands you numbers and makes you the analyst. This one explains — number → meaning → action — and discovers what's worth a human's eye. A deliberate, billed-by-design model call, on aggregates, admin-gated, cached.

That registry is the thing that lets us "easily think of how to use the data" — because every new thought is already expressible the moment we have the words for it.

04 — The core, live

Any question is a composition.

Pick the pieces. The question writes itself — and the interpreter tells you whose problem it is.

05 — The claim

Why this is world-class, and where the honesty line is.

The claim is specific: the near-zero marginal cost of a new question, with the interpretation included. That's the thing teams with far bigger data departments still don't have — because they solved the answer (dashboards) and never solved the question. An engine plus an interpreter means the thinking scales without the building scaling — the only way a team this size out-insights teams ten times larger.

And the honesty line, stated so we never oversell it: this is an analyst in the box over our own data, not an oracle. It reasons over aggregates, proposes hypotheses, points a human at what to look at. It does not assert causation it can't see — it removes the grind that currently stops the judgement call from being made at all.

06 — Future-proof

The vocabulary grows. The architecture doesn't.

Every foreseeable change is additive to the registry, not a rebuild.

  • VAD lands (the execution/prosody axis) → new metrics in the registry. The engine is untouched; behavioural-first today, prosody-rich tomorrow, same engine.
  • A real school arrives → a new entity and frame level; sovereignty and k-anonymity already handle it — they were designed for exactly this.
  • A new audience — learner, tutor, admin, government — is a different lens on one substrate, not a new product.
  • The interface never needs redesigning as the data grows, because the interface is language. The surface area can 10× and the way you interact with it does not change.
07 — The discipline

A commitment, not a boil-the-ocean.

The danger with a vision this size is that it becomes an excuse to build a platform before a feature. It must not. The discipline is Measuring Progress's Principle 5 — better × simpler × cheaper, and never build a signal before its consumer exists — turned on the engine itself.

  • Commit to the approach now. Every board from here is an instance of the engine — registry-defined, never a one-off page.
  • Start tiny. Five metrics, two frames, one explain call, over the raw tables that already exist. The registry can be five entries on day one and fifty by VAD-time.
  • Earn each addition. A new metric, the rollup tables, the prosody axis — each lands only when a consumer needs it.

Hold the architecture firmly and the surface area loosely.

08 — Inherited guarantees

IP and sovereignty, enforced once at the substrate.

Because every surface is an instance of one engine, the protections are enforced once and inherited everywhere.

  • IP guardrail. The telemetry is behaviour over content-IDs, never the generative method. What played, in what order, with what friction — yes; why (triple-helix, spaced-rep, adaptation) lives in code, and content sits behind the entitlement-gated audio proxy. Even a full export is one realised path, not the rules that generate paths — it can't reconstruct the course or the method.
  • Sovereignty. Every comparison is entity-vs-aggregate, never a named peer, with a k-anonymity floor so a band of one or two can't leak an individual. The interpreter sees cohorts, never a name.

Internally, for SSi admin, the engine is fully generous — it's our content and our learners — but built so that even an internal tool cannot become an external leak.

09 — For discussion

Open questions for Aran.

The real forks

  1. LLM-from-the-start, or substrate-first? Wire an explain call into the very first board — proving the world-class shape immediately — or prove the substrate on two or three boards and add the interpreter once there's something worth interpreting?
  2. How far beyond admin? The same engine could serve the learner's self-vs-cohort view and the tutor's needs-attention list. Design for that from day one, or admin-only first and generalise later?
  3. The first five metrics. Which five questions matter enough to define first? (Candidate: retention, content-friction, days-active, trial→paid, audio-failed.)
  4. Build vs buy the semantic layer. Hand-roll a tiny metric registry, or adopt a metrics-layer convention? (Lean: hand-roll tiny — the registry's content is the value, not the framework.)
